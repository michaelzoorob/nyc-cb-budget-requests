# Plain-language descriptions for the follow-up letters

The dashboard drafts letters from a community board to city agencies and elected officials about the board's budget requests. The letters used to quote the board's request and the agency's response word for word, which reads like a machine wrote it. You will write short plain-language descriptions that the letters use instead.

Each record has an `id`, a `kind` (`response`, `request` or `omb`) and the text to describe. Write one description per record.

## How the letters use your text

A letter reads like this, with your text in the braces.

> On behalf of Queens Community Board 2, I am writing to follow up on one of our FY2027 budget requests. We asked your agency to {request}.
>
> Your agency {response}. In the Executive Budget, OMB {omb}.

"Your agency" may instead be the agency's name ("The Department of Transportation"), and the letter may go to an elected official instead of the agency. Your text must read correctly in all of these.

- A **request** description is a verb phrase in the base form that completes "asked the agency to ...", such as "build catch basins at ...". Don't start it with "to".
- A **response** or **omb** description starts with "said" and completes "The agency ..." or "OMB ...", such as "said it doesn't support the request and gave no reason".

## Rules for every description

- Say what the text says, in plain words, in one sentence, joining points with "and that" where you can. Use two sentences only when one would run too long. The second sentence then starts with "It also said" or with "It" and a verb ("It offers free workshops ..."), never with a bare "It said". The letter adds the final period, so don't end with one.
- Don't use quotation marks, colons, semicolons, parentheses or dashes between words. Hyphens inside words are fine ("City-funded", "after-school").
- Use contractions where natural (doesn't, can't, isn't, won't). Prefer plain words to bureaucratic ones: "fund" or "pay for" rather than "allocate resources", "do" or "handle" rather than "accommodate" (except in the fixed disposition phrases below).
- "it" and "its" mean the agency that responded. "we", "us" and "our" mean the community board that made the request, which is the board writing the letter. It can be any of the city's 59 boards. When a response names a community board by number ("CB 4", "Community Board 11"), that is almost always the board that made the request, so write "we", "us" or "our board" instead of the number. Name any other agency, office or official in full the first time ("the Department of Transportation", "the Borough President", "our Council Member"). Common acronyms are fine (MTA, NYCHA, NYPD, FDNY, OMB, DOT).
- Keep every fact that matters, including reasons, conditions, amounts, dates, fiscal years, project and program names, places, and any office or person the text says to contact. Keep numbers as written.
- Add nothing the text doesn't say. Don't guess reasons or causes, don't soften or strengthen a commitment, and don't editorialize ("unfortunately", "we understand", "we appreciate"). Use "because" only when the text gives a fact as the reason for its answer, and don't link other facts with "since", "so" or "as a result".
- Leave out web addresses, email addresses and phone numbers, and say what they're for instead ("said we should apply through its online application", "said we should call the Transit Authority for the status"). The letter adds contact details itself.
- Leave out "Please contact the agency directly and promptly for more information" and similar stock lines when the response says more, since the letter already asks whom to contact. When the stock line is all the response says besides its disposition, keep it as the Responses section below shows. Keep a contact instruction when it names a specific office, person or program.
- Spell out street abbreviations (Blvd as Boulevard, Ave as Avenue, St as Street), fix obvious typos and don't copy ALL CAPS. Keep proper names as written.
- Keep it short. A response or omb description should stay under about 250 characters and a request description under about 200. When the text is long, keep the points that matter for what the board does next.

## Responses

A response often opens with one of DCP's dispositions. Use these words for them, then add what else the agency said.

| Disposition | Write |
| --- | --- |
| Agency supports and can accommodate | said it supports the request and can accommodate it |
| Agency supports but cannot accommodate | said it supports the request but can't accommodate it |
| Agency does not support and cannot accommodate | said it doesn't support the request |
| Agency does not support but can address the need alternatively | said it doesn't support the request as written but can address the need another way |
| This request has already been completed | said the request has already been completed |
| The agency does not understand the request as written and requires more clarification | said it doesn't understand the request as written and needs more detail |

When a negative disposition comes with no reason, end with "and gave no reason", as in "said it doesn't support the request and gave no reason". When its only other words are a stock "contact the agency" line, say so instead, as in "said it doesn't support the request and asked us to contact it directly for more information". When a response says nothing except to contact the agency, write "said we should contact it directly for more information".

Older responses sometimes add "; remove request" or "; resubmit request" and an "Explanation:" label. Ignore "remove request", "resubmit request" and the label, and describe the explanation.

Examples:

| Response | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Agency does not support and cannot accommodate. PLEASE CONTACT THE AGENCY DIRECTLY AND PROMPTLY FOR MORE INFORMATION. | said it doesn't support the request and asked us to contact it directly for more information |
| Further study by the agency of this request is needed. | said it needs to study the request further |
| Department of Parks and Recreation funds are insufficient for this project. We recommend this project be brought to the attention of your elected officials, i.e. Borough President and/or City Council member. (from Parks) | said it doesn't have the money for this project and suggested we bring it to our elected officials, such as the Borough President or our Council Member |
| Agency supports but cannot accommodate. NYC DOT does not have capital funding to support this request, contact your local elected officials for funding. (from DOT) | said it supports the request but doesn't have capital funding for it and suggested we ask our elected officials for funding |
| This request has already been completed. HRA continues to maintain funding for Community Food Connection (CFC; formerly EFAP). Note that, in general, pantries are funded by a wide variety of funding sources. ... We direct your attention to the eligibility criteria and application for becoming a Community Food Connection (CFC) provider https://www.nyc.gov/... | said the request has already been completed and that it still funds its Community Food Connection program, which food pantries can apply to join |
| Agency supports but cannot accommodate. More information is required by DCLA to consider this request, specifically each request must be tied to one specific eligible organization. | said it supports the request but can't accommodate it and needs each request tied to one eligible organization before it can consider it |
| This request is for the Parks department. (from the Department of Cultural Affairs) | said the request belongs with the Department of Parks and Recreation |

## Requests

A request record has the board's title, its explanation, the request type, the agency and the board. Describe what the board asked the agency to do, as a verb phrase that completes "asked the agency to ...". Most requests fit one verb, such as fund, build, install, repair, replace, study, hire, add, expand, keep, restore, create, clean, plant or reconstruct.

- Name the specific thing and place, such as streets, parks, schools, buildings and neighborhoods. Say "our district" for the board's own district.
- Include the board's reason only when it fits in a short clause and helps ("which flood regularly", "for older adults who can't reach a senior center").
- When the title and explanation disagree, follow the explanation. When both are vague, describe what's there without inventing detail ("fund more services for older adults in our district").
- Don't name the agency again. The letter names it right before your text.

Examples:

| Title and explanation | Description |
| --- | --- |
| Other capital budget request for DEP. Build catch basins around 7 train entrances on Queens Blvd where flooding is prevalent. The crosswalks leading up to 46th street, 40th street and 33rd Street, 7 train stations experience regular flooding but lack catch basins or infrastructure to relieve the flooding. | build catch basins at the crosswalks leading to the 7 train stations at 33rd, 40th and 46th Streets on Queens Boulevard, which flood regularly |
| Increasing discharge into the East River should not be an option. Supplement the Newtown Creek section of the Long Term Control Plan (LTCP) with additional measures to reduce the amount of raw sewage discharged into Newtown Creek beyond the LTCP plan, which will still allow hundreds of millions of gallons of raw sewage to discharge into the creek annually. | add measures to the Newtown Creek part of its Long Term Control Plan that cut raw sewage discharges into the creek beyond what the plan now allows |
| New food pantry in CB2. Establish a city sponsored food pantry in the CB 2 district in partnership with Sunnyside Community services and woodside on the move in order to address growing food insecurity and mediate the impact of cuts to snap. | open a City-sponsored food pantry in our district with Sunnyside Community Services and Woodside on the Move, to address growing food insecurity |

## OMB text

These are OMB's Executive Budget responses that a letter would otherwise quote. The letter reads "In the Executive Budget, OMB {description}", so here "it" means OMB. Call the agency that responded "the agency" (or name it), and don't repeat "in the Executive Budget". The other rules apply.

| OMB text | Description |
| --- | --- |
| The Department's fleet already includes vehicles of this type; the deployment of these resources is determined by the Department. | said the agency's fleet already includes vehicles of this type and that the agency decides where to deploy them |
| OMB supports the agency's recommendation to fund this expense budget request in FY 2023. | said it supports the agency's recommendation to fund this request in FY2023 |
| This capital project is included within an agency lump sum for FY 2021. Check the Commitment Plan. | said the project is funded within the agency's lump sum for FY2021 and that we should check the Commitment Plan |
| The Washington Heights corner project is not related to H+H (from NYC Health + Hospitals) | said the Washington Heights corner project isn't related to NYC Health + Hospitals |

## Output

Read your chunk 50 records at a time with the Read tool's offset and limit (one record per line). After each 50, write a JSON array to `WORK_DIR/out/<chunk>_pNN.json` (p01, p02, and so on), with one object per record in the same order.

    [{"id": "26ea05a7f8b8", "text": "said it needs to study the request further"}, ...]

Every id in your chunk must appear exactly once across your output files. Don't write any other files, and don't start other agents.
